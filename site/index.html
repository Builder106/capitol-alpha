Event day −250

Do members of Congress beat the market?

No.

Across 4,456 stock purchases members made from 2020 to 2025, copying each trade once it was disclosed earned nothing beyond what the market, size, value and momentum explain.

An event study by Olayinka Vaughan, tested under a plan written before any return was computed. Updated November 2026.

Scroll to walk through event time

Days −250 to −30

First, what each stock should have done

221trading days of history, fit for every stock

For every purchase, a model is fit on the stock's daily returns from 250 to 30 trading days before the event. It predicts each day's return from four factors:

  • The market
  • Small against large firms
  • Value against growth
  • Momentum

The abnormal return is the actual return minus that prediction. If members know something the market does not, it shows up here.

Day −9, in the median case

The trade

4,456purchases matched to CRSP common stock

A member, a spouse or a dependent buys a stock. The study covers 4,456 purchases in 2020 to 2025 that match a common stock in CRSP, the standard academic price database.

Most claims that Congress beats the market start the clock here. Nobody outside the household can see the trade yet, so a return measured from this day is one the public could never have earned.

Days −9 to 0

The filing

13days, the median wait before a House member reported a trade

The STOCK Act gives members at most 45 days to report a trade. House members took a median of 13 days. Of 1,965 House filings, 69 listed a trade reported after 45 days.

On the plot, the dots leave for day 0 in the order members filed.

Day 0

Disclosure: now anyone can copy it

3,942purchases a follower could have copied

From here the trade is public. The test asks what a follower earns by buying the same stock the next trading day. 3,942 of the purchases have a disclosure date, all from the House; the Senate records used here carry no filing date.

Each dot on the plot stands for one of them. From here they spread out by how each did against the model, and the shaded bands hold the middle half and the middle 80%. The blue line is their average.

Day +62, the primary test

The test chosen in advance

By day 62, one purchase in ten has gained more than 16% against the model and one in ten has lost nearly 18%.

The plot now zooms in 18 times, past that spread, to the average and its 95% band.

The plan named one test before the data came in: buy every disclosed purchase on the first trading day after disclosure, hold it for 62 trading days, and measure the portfolio's four-factor alpha with Newey-West errors.

Day +125

Six months on, still at zero

−0.68%average at day 125, inside its 95% band

On every one of the 126 days after disclosure, the 95% band covers zero.

The average reaches −0.68% at 125 days. With six tests run, the Holm-adjusted p-value is 0.34.

Measured from the trade date instead, which the public cannot act on, the answer is the same. None of the three tested horizons shows a significant abnormal return after adjustment.

What changed from the class version

This page first went up in spring 2025 as a course project for QAC 420 at Wesleyan, and it reported that members' purchases beat the S&P 500. An audit in November 2026 found that the result did not hold. A text filter had dropped every House purchase, leaving about 220 Senate trades. Only the 50 most-traded stocks had prices. Returns started on the trade date, and the "alpha" assumed every stock moved one for one with the market.

The redo fixes the sample, the prices and the model, and its plan was committed before any new return was computed. One change came after seeing results: the first run also subtracted each stock's pre-event trend, which the plan does not call for. That run is still printed by the code and reported in the results, so the change can be checked.

Data, method and limits

Trades
House and Senate periodic transaction reports, scraped past the House site's 50-row page limit and parsed from PDFs.
Prices
CRSP daily returns through WRDS, through 31 December 2025, delisting returns included. CRSP data is licensed and is not published here.
Model
Fama-French three factors plus momentum from Kenneth French's data library.
Inference
Newey-West errors for the portfolio, errors clustered by member and month for event averages, Holm correction across the six event tests.
The dots
One dot per disclosed purchase. From day 0 the dots sit at the percentiles of that day's abnormal returns, so each shows where one purchase in 3,942 falls, not a particular trade. Later days have fewer purchases with prices, and the dots spread over those.
Limits
Only House trades have disclosure dates, so the primary test is House only. The trade records were collected in April 2026, so some filings for late 2025 trades are missing. This is research, not investment advice.
