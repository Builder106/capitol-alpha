# Where to submit the paper

Notes on journals that accept AI-assisted work from an undergraduate author,
and on Zenodo's own AI rules. Researched 2026-11-12. Publisher-wide policies
and the student journals' public pages were checked; each journal's full
author guidelines were not, and a journal can be stricter than its
publisher.

## Summary

AI-assisted papers are widely allowed if the AI is not listed as an author and
its use is disclosed. For an independent undergraduate, the real limits are
who may submit, exclusivity rules, and fees. Most student journals publish no
AI policy, so ask the editors before submitting.

## Zenodo

Zenodo's generative AI policy for depositors was published on 2026-04-27.

- AI may be used "as a tool, but not as the source of the research itself."
  Acceptable uses include language editing, code generation the depositor
  reviews, and literature summaries checked against the sources.
- Zenodo bans content produced largely by AI "without a verifiable research
  basis": raw AI output posted as a paper, AI-written survey papers, and
  fabricated results. It enforces this; one user had more than 850 records
  removed.
- AI tools cannot be listed as creators or contributors. The depositor
  "takes full responsibility for all of its contents, regardless of how
  those contents were produced."
- Zenodo expects substantive AI use to be disclosed in the record's
  description: which tools, for what, and which parts of the work.
  "Non-disclosure on its own is not grounds for removal."
- If a disclosure goes in the paper, `zenodo.json` needs a matching sentence
  in its `description`.

This paper rests on real CRSP data, code that can be rerun, and a plan with a
dated commit history, so it falls on the allowed side.

## Undergraduate economics journals

| Journal | Who can submit | AI policy | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Undergraduate Economic Review (Illinois Wesleyan) | Any undergraduate who did in-depth economics research; you can submit after graduating if most of the work was done as an undergraduate | None published | No fees (per a third-party source). Mostly empirical papers. Exclusive submission. Submissions were due to reopen in fall 2026, and the journal does not review from late April to August. |
| Issues in Political Economy (Elon / Mary Washington) | Undergraduate work submitted through a faculty sponsor | None published | Student-edited, double-blind review. The project began as a Wesleyan QAC 420 course project, so a QAC professor could sponsor it. |

## Professional journals

| Publisher | AI rule |
| --- | --- |
| Elsevier (e.g. Finance Research Letters) | AI may help with writing. A declaration is required at submission, placed before the references. Substantive changes must be declared; spelling fixes need not be. No AI-made images. |
| Taylor & Francis (e.g. Applied Economics Letters) | A "Declaration of generative AI use" giving the tool's name and version, how and why it was used, confirmation that its terms of use were checked, and that the authors take full responsibility, including for the references. No AI-made images, figures or data. |
| AEA journals | AI cannot be an author. Any use, including drafting or editing text, must be briefly described at submission. |

These are a much harder bar than student journals, but their AI rules do not
exclude this paper.

## A journal for null results

The Series of Unsurprising Results in Economics (SURE), University of
Canterbury, publishes careful studies with insignificant results. It is open
access with no submission fee. It only takes papers already rejected by an
EconLit-indexed journal where the main criticism was that the result was
unsurprising; the rejection letter and referee reports go in with the
submission. That makes it a sensible next step if a regular journal turns the
paper down for being a null result. No published AI policy was found.

## Open questions

- Does a Zenodo working paper count as prior publication? UER requires that
  the paper not be "published in another journal". Ask before posting.
  Large publishers generally allow preprints; confirm in the journal's own
  instructions.
- Is the paper independent research? If it grew out of QAC 420, the course
  and Wesleyan's AI policy bear on the declarations and possibly the
  acknowledgments.
